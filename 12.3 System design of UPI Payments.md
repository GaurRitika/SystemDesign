# UPI System Design — How UPI Works

## Introduction

UPI (Unified Payments Interface) has revolutionized digital payments in India by enabling instant bank-to-bank money transfers using a Virtual Payment Address (VPA).

Unlike traditional banking systems that require account numbers, IFSC codes, and branch details, UPI simplifies the payment process into a seamless experience.

Applications such as **Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, and BHIM** use the UPI infrastructure to enable instant payments.

This document explains the **high-level architecture of UPI** and how a UPI transaction is processed.

---

## Before UPI: Traditional Banking System

Before UPI, digital money transfers were primarily performed through banking systems.

Examples of banks include:

* ICICI Bank
* HDFC Bank
* State Bank of India (SBI)
* Axis Bank
* Yes Bank

These banks operate under regulations established by the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**.

For example, if a customer wanted to transfer money from an ICICI Bank account to an HDFC Bank account, they traditionally needed:

* Account Number
* Bank Name
* Branch
* IFSC Code
* Transfer Amount

These details were required to uniquely identify the destination bank account.

---

# Traditional Payment Mechanisms

Before UPI, multiple payment mechanisms were commonly used.

## 1. IMPS — Immediate Payment Service

IMPS was designed for relatively smaller and urgent transfers.

Characteristics:

* Near-instant transfers
* Useful for smaller amounts
* Money generally reflects quickly in the recipient's account

Example:

```text
₹500
₹1,000
₹5,000
```

---

## 2. NEFT — National Electronic Funds Transfer

NEFT is used for transferring money between bank accounts.

Historically, NEFT operated through batch-based settlement windows, so transactions were not always reflected immediately.

Users could share the **UTR (Unique Transaction Reference)** after initiating a transfer.

---

## 3. RTGS — Real Time Gross Settlement

RTGS is primarily used for high-value transactions.

Example:

```text
₹5,00,000
₹10,00,000
₹20,00,000
```

Unlike traditional batch-based transfers, RTGS processes transactions individually in real time.

---

# Problems with Traditional Banking

Traditional bank transfers required users to enter multiple pieces of information:

* Account Number
* Bank Name
* IFSC Code
* Branch Details

This created several problems:

1. Long account details were difficult to remember.
2. Users could make typing mistakes.
3. Payments could take time to settle.
4. The overall process was not very user-friendly.

UPI was designed to simplify this experience.

---

# What is UPI?

**UPI = Unified Payments Interface**

The fundamental idea behind UPI was to make digital payments extremely simple.

Instead of entering complete bank account details, a user can initiate a payment using a **UPI ID / Virtual Payment Address (VPA)**.

A typical payment requires:

* Recipient's UPI ID
* Amount
* UPI PIN for authentication

The transaction can then be processed almost instantly.

---

# NPCI — The Backbone of UPI

The UPI ecosystem is operated through infrastructure provided by the **National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI)**.

NPCI is:

* Not a payment application
* Not a traditional bank
* Not simply a payment gateway

Instead, NPCI provides the infrastructure and switching layer that enables participating banks to communicate and route UPI transactions.

A simplified representation is:

```text
                    NPCI
                     |
             Secure UPI Network
                     |
       --------------------------------
       |        |        |        |
      ICICI    HDFC     SBI    Yes Bank
```

NPCI operates within a controlled and trusted ecosystem.

Applications do not simply make arbitrary public API calls to NPCI.

---

# Trusted Banking Network

Banks participating in UPI connect to the NPCI infrastructure.

Examples include:

* ICICI Bank
* HDFC Bank
* SBI
* Axis Bank
* Yes Bank

Payment applications such as Google Pay and PhonePe do not directly connect to NPCI as ordinary public clients.

Instead, they work with participating partner banks.

Simplified architecture:

```text
Google Pay
     |
Partner Bank
     |
    NPCI
```

and:

```text
PhonePe
     |
Partner Bank
     |
    NPCI
```

---

# Payment Service Providers (PSPs)

Applications such as:

* Google Pay
* PhonePe
* Paytm

provide the user-facing payment experience.

They provide functionality such as:

* User interface
* QR code scanning
* UPI ID entry
* Payment initiation
* Authentication screens
* Transaction status

The application itself does not directly perform the underlying bank-to-bank settlement.

The banking infrastructure performs the actual debit and credit operations.

---

# Partner Banks

Because the UPI network operates through participating banks, payment applications work with partner banks.

For example:

```text
PhonePe
   |
Yes Bank
   |
NPCI
```

A payment application therefore acts as the user-facing layer while the partner bank provides connectivity into the banking/UPI ecosystem.

---

# Virtual Payment Address (VPA)

One of the most important concepts introduced by UPI is the **Virtual Payment Address (VPA)**.

Instead of remembering:

```text
Account Number
IFSC Code
Branch
Bank Name
```

a user can use a UPI ID such as:

```text
ritika@okhdfcbank
john@ybl
user@oksbi
```

A VPA generally contains:

```text
Username + UPI Handle
```

Example:

```text
ritika@okhdfcbank
```

### Advantages

* Easy to remember
* Easy to share
* No need to manually enter IFSC
* No need to manually enter branch details
* Simplifies payment initiation

---

# QR Codes

UPI also supports QR-code-based payments.

A QR code can encode the payment information required to initiate a UPI transaction, commonly including the recipient's UPI ID and, for merchant payments, additional payment information.

Instead of manually entering a UPI ID, the user can simply scan the QR code.

Conceptually:

```text
QR Code
   |
   ▼
Payment Information / VPA
   |
   ▼
Payment App
```

QR scanning therefore acts as a convenient way to provide the recipient/payment details.

---

# Creating a Payment

Suppose User A wants to send ₹1,000 to User B.

The payment application creates a payment request containing information such as:

```text
From:
user1@yesbank

To:
piyush@okicici

Amount:
₹1,000
```

This represents the user's **payment intent**.

The user is essentially saying:

```text
I want to transfer ₹1,000
from my account
to this recipient.
```

# Complete UPI Transaction Flow

## Step 1 — User Opens the Payment Application

The user opens an application such as:

* PhonePe
* Google Pay
* Paytm
* BHIM

---

## Step 2 — User Provides Recipient Information

The user can:

* Scan a QR code
* Enter a UPI ID
* Select a saved recipient

For example:

```text
Recipient:
piyush@okicici

Amount:
₹1,000
```

---

## Step 3 — Payment Intent is Created

The application creates the payment request.

Conceptually:

```text
Sender
   |
   | ₹1,000
   |
   ▼
Recipient
```

---

## Step 4 — PSP Communicates with Its Partner Bank

The payment application forwards the transaction through its banking partner.

Example:

```text
PhonePe
   |
   ▼
Yes Bank
```

---

## Step 5 — Partner Bank Sends the Request Through NPCI

The participating bank communicates with the NPCI UPI infrastructure.

```text
PhonePe
   |
   ▼
Yes Bank
   |
   ▼
NPCI
```

NPCI receives the transaction request through the trusted banking ecosystem.

---

## Step 6 — Recipient Bank is Identified

NPCI determines which bank is associated with the recipient's UPI address.

For example:

```text
Recipient VPA
      |
      ▼
  ICICI Bank
```

The transaction therefore needs to involve the recipient's bank.

---

## Step 7 — Authentication

The user is prompted to authenticate the transaction.

Typically, this involves entering the **UPI PIN**.

```text
User
 |
 ▼
UPI PIN
 |
 ▼
Authentication
```

The transaction proceeds only after successful authentication.

---

## Step 8 — Sender Account is Debited

After successful authorization, the sender bank processes the debit.

Example:

```text
Sender Account

₹5,000
   |
   | Debit ₹1,000
   ▼
₹4,000
```

---

## Step 9 — Receiver Account is Credited

The recipient bank processes the corresponding credit.

```text
Receiver Account

₹2,000
   |
   | Credit ₹1,000
   ▼
₹3,000
```

---

## Step 10 — Transaction Confirmation

The participating systems exchange acknowledgements regarding the transaction status.

Once the required processing and confirmations are completed, the transaction is marked as successful.

The user then receives a confirmation through the payment application.

---

# Transaction Rollback / Failure Handling

A transaction can fail for several reasons.

For example:

* Receiver bank is unavailable
* Server timeout
* Network failure
* Missing acknowledgement
* Technical failure during processing

Consider this scenario:

```text
Sender Bank
     |
     | Debit ₹1,000
     ▼
Money Debited
     |
     ▼
Receiver Bank
     |
     X
No confirmation
```

The sender may initially see:

```text
₹1,000 Debited
```

However, if the transaction cannot be successfully completed, the system performs the necessary reversal/refund process.

The user may eventually receive:

```text
₹1,000 Refunded
```

This is why users sometimes experience:

```text
Amount Debited
       ↓
Transaction Failed
       ↓
Amount Reversed / Refunded
```

# Push Payments

A **push payment** occurs when the sender initiates the payment.

Example:

```text
User A
  |
  | Send ₹500
  ▼
User B
```

The sender actively pushes money to the recipient.

This is the common "Send Money" flow.

---

# Pull / Collect Payments

A **pull/collect payment** occurs when the recipient requests money from the sender.

Example:

```text
User B
  |
  | Request ₹1,000
  ▼
User A
```

The sender receives the request and can authorize it.

This is commonly referred to as a **collect/request-money flow**.

---

# UPI Participants

A UPI transaction involves several different participants.

## 1. Payer

The person initiating the payment.

```text
Payer → Sends Money
```

---

## 2. Payer PSP

The payment application used by the payer.

Examples:

* Google Pay
* PhonePe
* Paytm

It provides the user interface and initiates the transaction.

---

## 3. Payer PSP Bank / Partner Bank

The bank through which the PSP connects into the UPI ecosystem.

---

## 4. Issuing / Remitter Bank

The bank associated with the payer's account from which money is debited.

---

## 5. NPCI

Provides the UPI switching and routing infrastructure.

---

## 6. Beneficiary / Receiver Bank

The bank associated with the recipient's account.

It is responsible for crediting the recipient.

---

## 7. Payee PSP

The payment application associated with the recipient, where applicable.

---

# High-Level Participant Flow

```text
Payer
  |
  ▼
Payer PSP
  |
  ▼
Payer PSP Bank
  |
  ▼
NPCI
  |
  ▼
Receiver Bank
  |
  ▼
Receiver
```

Depending on the transaction and participants involved, additional PSP/bank interactions may occur.

# Roles and Responsibilities

| Component        | Responsibility                                              |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| User / Payer     | Initiates payment                                           |
| Payer PSP        | Provides UI and initiates payment                           |
| PSP Bank         | Provides connectivity into UPI                              |
| NPCI             | Routes and processes UPI transactions                       |
| Remitter Bank    | Debits the payer's account                                  |
| Beneficiary Bank | Credits the receiver's account                              |
| Payee PSP        | Provides recipient-side payment experience where applicable |
| VPA              | Provides a user-friendly payment address                    |
| UPI PIN          | Authenticates the transaction                               |

# Why UPI is Powerful

The biggest advantage of UPI is that it abstracts away the complexity of traditional banking.

Traditional transfer:

```text
Account Number
      +
IFSC
      +
Bank Name
      +
Branch
      +
Amount
```

UPI:

```text
VPA
 +
Amount
 +
UPI PIN
```

The underlying banking infrastructure remains complex, but the user experience becomes extremely simple.

# UPI as a Distributed System

At a high level, UPI is an example of a large-scale distributed payment system.

It needs to coordinate:

* Multiple banks
* Multiple payment applications
* Millions of users
* Real-time transactions
* Authentication
* Debit operations
* Credit operations
* Transaction status
* Failures and reversals
* Network communication
* High availability

The system must continue operating even when individual components experience failures.

This makes reliability and fault tolerance extremely important.

---

# Why Failure Handling Matters

Imagine:

```text
Sender Bank
     |
     | ₹1,000
     ▼
NPCI
     |
     ▼
Receiver Bank
```

What happens if:

```text
Sender Bank → SUCCESS
Receiver Bank → TIMEOUT
```

The system cannot simply assume that the transaction failed.

It needs mechanisms for:

* Transaction status tracking
* Acknowledgements
* Timeouts
* Reconciliation
* Reversal
* Retry handling
* Idempotency
* Final transaction state

These mechanisms are essential in any large-scale financial system.

# Internal NPCI Implementation

The publicly available information primarily explains the **high-level architecture** of UPI.

The exact internal implementation of NPCI is not publicly documented in sufficient detail to reconstruct its complete production architecture.

Questions such as:

* How exactly are transactions routed internally?
* What databases are used?
* What messaging infrastructure is used?
* How is horizontal scaling implemented?
* How are internal failures handled?
* What exact services run inside NPCI?
* How are internal transaction states maintained?
* What infrastructure is used for disaster recovery?

are difficult to answer definitively using public information.

Therefore, any detailed internal architecture beyond publicly documented information should be treated as an approximation rather than an official description.

# Engineering Challenges

A system like UPI must solve extremely difficult engineering problems.

### 1. Scalability

The system needs to handle enormous transaction volumes.

### 2. Low Latency

Payments are expected to complete within seconds.

### 3. Fault Tolerance

Individual banks, services, or networks can temporarily fail.

### 4. Consistency

Money cannot simply disappear or be duplicated.

### 5. Reliability

Financial transactions require extremely high reliability.

### 6. Security

Authentication and authorization must protect users and transactions.

### 7. Reconciliation

Transactions across different systems must eventually be reconciled correctly.

### 8. Availability

The infrastructure needs to remain available despite failures in individual components.

# Final Takeaways

* **UPI stands for Unified Payments Interface.**
* **NPCI provides the core UPI infrastructure.**
* Payment applications provide the user-facing experience.
* PSPs work with participating banks to access the UPI ecosystem.
* **VPA** provides a simpler way to identify a payment destination.
* QR codes provide a convenient way to enter payment information.
* The sender is authenticated using a UPI PIN.
* The sender's bank performs the debit.
* The receiver's bank performs the credit.
* NPCI provides the routing/switching layer between participating entities.
* Transaction acknowledgements and failure-handling mechanisms are essential.
